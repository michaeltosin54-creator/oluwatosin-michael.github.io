HR project from my AJID training

During my AJID HR training I built an end-to-end toolkit for the employee lifecycle, from hiring to offboarding, aimed at an HR Generalist or People Operations role.

This is a practice project for a fictional remote-first technology company. The results and dashboard figures are mock data and expected outcomes, not real company results.

Challenge

A fast-growing remote-first tech firm had unstructured workflows, hiring managers using different criteria, 25% candidate drop-off during interviews and 18% turnover within 90 days because onboarding was inconsistent.

Action

I designed one standard framework for the whole employee lifecycle: compliance guidelines, a candidate scoring matrix, structured interview guides, a 30-60-90 day onboarding plan and data trackers.

Expected result

Lower operational risk, no offboarding errors, time-to-hire cut by about 15%, and metrics the company can track as it grows.

Recruitment and talent acquisition

Job description: Junior People Operations Specialist

Remote (US/Canada), People Team. The role is the backbone of daily HR operations: onboarding, HRIS data integrity, compliance monitoring and benefits support.

  • Coordinate weekly new-hire cohorts, including IT provisioning and payroll.
  • Keep employee records accurate and current in the HRIS (BambooHR or Workday).
  • Act as first contact for questions on policies, benefits and paid time off.

Structured interview guide

Problem solving and adaptability. Tell me about a time you managed several tight deadlines and a sudden change disrupted your plan. Strong answers show prioritising by business urgency, telling stakeholders about timeline changes, and staying calm.

Discretion and confidentiality. An employee asks if their name is on an unannounced layoff list that you can access. Strong answers set a clear boundary without getting defensive, follow privacy rules, and point the person to the official communications channel.

Onboarding and offboarding

30-60-90 day new-hire checklist

Days 1 to 30, learn. Day 1: payroll and I-9/W-4 verification, HRIS profile, team welcome. Week 1: IT hardware, credentials and benefits enrolment. Day 30: review procedures and pass the foundational modules.

Days 31 to 60, contribute. Week 5: move from shadowing to doing low-risk recurring weekly updates alone. Day 60: co-deliver a project with a senior mentor.

Days 61 to 90, own. Week 9: take full ownership of an independent workflow. Day 90: complete the probationary review.

Offboarding workflow

Single sign-on, company email and database access are revoked automatically at 5:00 PM local time on the last day. Prepaid shipping containers are sent so hardware comes back within 5 business days.

Exit interview questions

  • What were the main reasons you decided to leave?
  • Did you have clear targets and the resources to do your job?
  • What is your view of your supervisor’s leadership style, and what would you change?
Performance management and policy samples

Performance improvement plan (sample)

Example case: an Account Executive (John Doe, managed by Jane Smith) on a 60-day plan. Current output is 10 calls a week and 1 lead a month; the target is 40 calls a week and 4 leads a month.

  • Weekly 30-minute coaching sessions on the metrics.
  • Progress reviews at Day 30 and Day 60.
  • If targets are not met and sustained in 60 days, the matter escalates under the disciplinary process, up to separation.

Draft policy: remote work guidelines

  • Purpose: set performance and security standards for remote work.
  • Core hours: stay responsive from 9:00 AM to 3:00 PM EST.
  • Security: no unencrypted public networks, all access through the company VPN, and no local copies of personal or company data on personal devices.
